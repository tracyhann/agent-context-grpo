01 — the one axis that separates these methods

Everything differs on the grouping key

A group-relative policy gradient replaces a learned critic with a baseline computed from a set of comparable experiences. The entire design question is which experiences count as comparable — every method below is a different answer to that, and nothing else about them differs much.

GRPO2402.03300
x₀  →  group
Same task. One flat group per prompt; no notion of a step.
GiGPO2505.10978
s_t  →  group
Same current state, matched exactly. Anchor states bucket steps inside the rollout group.
HGPO2602.22817
(s_{t−k}, …, s_t)  →  group
Same exact k-step history. Nested buckets by depth; support decays fast in k.
G²PO2606.22995
o_t  →  graph node
Same observation. Occurrences become nodes in an observation-transition graph.
Oursthis note
(o_t, c_t)  —f_φ→  z_t  →  weighted neighbourhood
Same observation and similar action-conditioned future consequences. The observation bucket is refined, not replaced.

The first four prescribe equivalence by identity; the fifth infers it. Note ours still requires o_u = o_v as a hard gate — it only ever splits a G²PO bucket, never merges across observations.

02 — the executable MVP

Five equations

The credit oracle sits entirely outside this loop. Online, nothing in the grouping sees a reward, a return, or an advantage — that is what keeps the leave-one-out baseline valid.

1
C_x(o_u) = { v = (j, t′) : o_v = o_u , j ≠ i }
Candidate pool: same task, same observation, different trajectory. The j ≠ i exclusion is load-bearing — if v came from u's own rollout, G_v would lie downstream of a_u and the baseline would depend on the action it is meant to evaluate.
2
P_u = Lawa∼q [ μ̃_ψ⁻(χ_u, a) ] ,   H(q) ≥ H_min
Predicted-consequence distribution over probe actions, from a frozen, reward-free successor-feature model. The entropy floor is the fix in E1: with q = π the law collapses to a point mass as the policy sharpens, and the method silently reverts to G²PO.
3
dpred_uv = D(P_u, P_v)  over a shared action set, scale-free
revisedOriginally an action-set-free distributional distance. Two measured defects forced the change: probing each occurrence's own top-M makes this measure probe mismatch rather than context (E2), and raw distances are dominated by episode position rather than credit (E3).
4
w_uv = 1[o_u = o_v] · 1[i_u ≠ i_v] · exp( −dpred_uv / τ_b ) ,   τ_b = median dpred
Affinity, with w_uu = 0. The adaptive median bandwidth makes the weights invariant to global drift in ψ — but it is also why E[w] is a useless collapse diagnostic: being scale-invariant, it absorbs the very collapse it was meant to detect.
5
b_uLOO = Σj≠i α_uj Ḡ_u,j ⁄ Σj≠i α_uj ,   A_uCC = G_u − b_uLOO
Repeated visits are aggregated within each reference trajectory first, so the effective sample size counts trajectories, not correlated repeat visits. Total advantage: A_u = A_iEP + λ_CC · A_uCC.
revisedShrinkage was λ = f(n_eff) alone. That is backwards: concentrating weights onto genuinely comparable neighbours is the method's purpose, yet it lowers n_eff and so triggered fallback to G²PO. It measured the variance cost of conditioning and was blind to the bias benefit. Now an MSE-optimal trade-off, λ* = (ρB)² / [ (ρB)² + σ²(1/n_eff − 1/J) ], where B is the measured false-merge bias (0.26–0.36 sd) and ρ is confidence in the metric — the point where grouping uncertainty enters. ρ→0 collapses exactly to G²PO.
02b — one training step, end to end

The pipeline

What happens in each of the 150 steps of the matched-scale run — rollout on the left rail, then credit assignment forking into the episode-level and step-level estimators that sum into the PPO update.

1

Sample tasks & replicate into groups

Draw 18 ALFWorld games from the official train split; replicate each ×8 (same game, same start) → 144 concurrent episodes. The 8 siblings of a task are the comparison group — no critic anywhere.

2

Multi-turn rollout (≤50 turns each)

Per turn: prompt = task + last-2 history + observation + admissible actions → policy generates ≤768 tokens of <think>…</think><action>…</action> (the CoT itself is trained) → parser extracts the action → env returns reward (success 10 / invalid −0.1). Generation runs on the FSDP training model itself (HF path — vLLM was retired for this stack after the sm_120 forensics; see the fix ledger in RESULTS_0827). Sampling per the Qwen3 model card: temp 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 20 (val: 0.7/0.8/20).

3

Flatten to occurrences

Episodes break into ≈6,000 transitions u=(trajectory i, turn t), each carrying its observation text, its position in the trajectory, and its discounted return-to-go (γ=0.95).

4
episode level — shared with GRPO

AEP: group-relative return

Episode return standardised against the 7 siblings (mean_std_norm) and broadcast to the trajectory's tokens. This half is identical in the GRPO baseline arm.

step level — ours (ccpo)

ACC: context-conditioned step credit

Gate: occurrences with the same (task, observation text) share a bucket — G²PO's node.
Context: φ = frozen projection of obs-bag ‖ thermometer-coded {t, n_unique, revisit, progress}.
Baseline: leave-one-out over other trajectories, weighted exp(−d(φ)/τ_b), τ_b = bucket median.
Trust: λ* = (ρB)²⁄((ρB)² + σ²(1/n_eff − 1/J)) shrinks toward the uniform bucket mean when contexts can't be told apart.

Same screen, different history: at "You open the microwave 1…", the rollout that arrived at t=5 carrying the apple is not judged by the one that wandered in at t=23 empty-handed — its weight decays with context distance.
5

Combine & normalise

A = AEP + w·std-norm(ACC) with w=1; the step term is standardised per task so equal weight means equal scale (the family's mean_std_norm convention). Buckets with identical contexts collapse to the plain G²PO baseline by construction.

6

Token-level PPO update

~25 minibatches of 240 transitions: PPO clip on every response token, KL-to-reference penalty 0.01 with the log-ratio clamped to ±20 before exp (the NaN fix), grad-clip 1.0, lr 1e-6. FSDP updates the weights — and the same module generates the next step's rollouts (no engine, no weight sync).

7

Evaluate & checkpoint

Every 5 steps: 128 episodes on ALFWorld unseen (temp 0.4). Every 25 steps: checkpoint to results/verl_ccpo_alfworld/. Curves land in fig_verl_curves.png; the estimator's health prints every step as [ccpo] λ_u · n_eff · live · buckets.

02c — ACC in four pictures

Context-conditioned step credit, one figure per mechanism

The four moving parts of the step-level term, drawn on the worked example from the training run: three sibling rollouts of the task "put a clean plate in countertop" all reach the observation "You open the cabinet 3 … you see a plate 1 and a mug 2."

1 · gate — who shares a bucket
G²PO's node, kept as a hard gate: only occurrences with byte-identical observation text within one task's sibling group merge. One changed character (a taken plate) is a different bucket; other tasks never mix.
2 · context — the φ fingerprint
Thermometer coding makes φ-distance monotone in context difference: t=4 vs t=19 differ in 6 of 12 cells, t=4 vs t=5 in ≤1 — unlike hashed tags, where step 1 vs 28 looked no farther apart than 15 vs 16 (measured corr −0.187 → +0.92 after the fix). Inside a bucket the obs half is constant, so distances come purely from context.
3 · baseline — weighted leave-one-out
u's counterfactual "what do trajectories like mine earn from here" is dominated by its context twin and barely dented by the lost wanderer — and u's own outcome never judges its own action (the leakage in released G²PO code).
⊕ the weighting, worked in full — contexts, distances, weights, baseline

Step 1 — the contexts. Each occurrence's φ carries the trajectory scalars {t, n_unique, revisit, progress} (thermometer-coded) beside the shared observation half:

  • u (rollout 2, judged): {t: 4, n_unique: 5, revisit: 0, progress: 1.00} — direct route, plate in reach
  • rollout 5 (twin): {t: 4, n_unique: 5, revisit: 0, progress: 1.00}
  • rollout 7 (wanderer): {t: 19, n_unique: 9, revisit: 1, progress: 0.47} — 6 of 12 thermometer cells differ on t alone, plus the revisit bit and half the progress

Step 2 — distances → weights. Within the bucket the observation half of φ is identical, so distance is pure context. With τ_b = the bucket's median pairwise distance (scale-free):

d(u, r5) ≈ 0  → w₅ = e⁻⁰ = 1.00
d(u, r7) ≈ 1.5·τ_b → w₇ = e⁻¹·⁵ ≈ 0.22

Step 3 — leave-one-out baseline. u's own trajectory is excluded; the others' returns (G₅ = 0.8, G₇ = 0.1) combine under the weights:

b_LOO(u) = (1.00·0.8 + 0.22·0.1) / (1.00 + 0.22) = 0.822 / 1.22 = 0.67

Step 4 — the advantage (before λ* blending and per-task normalisation):

ACC(u) = G_u − b_LOO = 0.9 − 0.67 = +0.23

Contrast at the same moment — G²PO (uniform, self included): (0.9 + 0.8 + 0.1)/3 = 0.60 → +0.30, inflated by the doomed wanderer and by u grading its own work. GRPO: no step baseline at all — every step of rollout 2 inherits the episode-level +0.59. If the three contexts had been indistinguishable, λ* would return the estimate to the (no-self) uniform mean 0.45 — the conditioning is only spent where it can be cashed.

4 · trust — the λ* dial
A bias–variance arbiter, not a hyperparameter: conditioning is trusted exactly in proportion to the bias it can remove (ρB) versus the variance it costs (fewer effective neighbors). Empirically λ ≈ 0.9 in the matched-scale run's dense buckets and ≈ 0.05 on sparse small-scale ALFWorld — the same formula, both behaviors.
⊕ where uncertainty lives in the method — click to expand

Step 4 — the λ* trust dial is the method's single, load-bearing uncertainty mechanism: before the context-weighted baseline is used, λ* asks "how uncertain is this conditioned estimate relative to the bias it removes?" and blends accordingly:

λ* = (ρB)² / ( (ρB)² + σ²(1/n_eff − 1/J) )

Each symbol carries one species of uncertainty:

  • σ² — outcome uncertainty: within-bucket variance of the other trajectories' returns. Noisy neighbors → shakier conditioned baseline → λ shrinks.
  • n_eff — effective-sample uncertainty: weight concentration reduces how many neighbors you're really averaging (n_eff = (Σw)²/Σw²); 1/n_eff − 1/J is precisely the extra variance purchased by conditioning versus using the whole bucket.
  • ρ — metric uncertainty: confidence that φ-distances mean anything. An uninformative metric can't deliver the bias reduction, so ρ→0 collapses λ→0 (back to G²PO) no matter how the numbers look.
  • B — prior belief about the bias: the measured false-merge magnitude (0.30 sd) — how wrong the unconditioned baseline is expected to be.

So uncertainty isn't modeled as an explicit posterior — it's an MSE arbitration: trust conditioning exactly when expected-bias-removed² exceeds variance-added. Two supporting appearances elsewhere: τ_b (bucket-median bandwidth) normalizes distance-scale uncertainty away, and the entropy floor on the probe (§02's H(q) ≥ H_min) guards against distributional collapse of the fingerprint — but those are stabilizers, not the dial.

Project history: richer uncertainty machinery — evidential deep learning for the affinity, learned variance heads — was built and considered during the learned-components phase, and dropped when the whole learned stack failed to beat the frozen φ. The conclusion (recorded in idea.md): uncertainty belongs in the estimator arithmetic (λ*), not in a trained module that would itself need calibrating.

Evidence

Phases 0 and 1 were run end to end. Three of the four things that first looked like failures turned out to be measurement defects in the test, not properties of the hypothesis — each is recorded here with the correction it forced.

The probe must not sharpen with the policy

With q = π, both the sampling law and the centring anchor collapse onto a*, so P_u → δ₀ for every occurrence. Decoupling holds the spread.

Lead with bias, not coverage

The original pitch — exact matching doesn't scale — is false in these benchmarks. What is real is that exact matching is biased: same observation, different context, different credit.

coverage (≥3 traj) 46–78%

A trap worth keeping

In any same-observation grouping study, bucket size and bucket informativeness are anti-correlated. The largest bucket for a task is always the episode-start observation — every rollout shares it, and every occurrence in it has identical context because nothing has happened yet. Ranking candidate states by bucket size therefore selects against the effect you are trying to measure. In our first pass, 50% of pinned states sat at t = 0 and 13 of 24 pairs had no context difference at all.

05 — cost of the full training run

What the expensive experiment would cost

Two terms, both measured here: rollout latency per env step, and training throughput at 9,208 tokens/s/GPU for the 1.7B. Two results matter more than the totals — rollout is the small term (agentic RL backprops over long trajectories, so the gradient pass dominates and is identical for every method), and the scoring path is an implementation choice worth 20×.

Scoring the whole action set is cheaper than generating one action

Autoregressive decode is memory-bandwidth-bound: six sequential steps, each re-reading all 1.7B weights. Scoring is compute-bound: encode the shared prompt once, keep the KV cache, then one batched forward over 45 × 6 candidate tokens — two weight reads total, densely batched. And with exact π over the admissible set there is nothing left to generate; the action is sampled from the categorical directly.

The 7.5× penalty is therefore not the method. It is the cost of scoring through an HTTP endpoint that re-encodes the 372-token prompt once per candidate — 17,001 tokens where 642 suffice. Realising the 0.37× needs shared-prefix scoring, which vLLM's OpenAI API does not expose but which is natural inside a verl-style trainer that owns the forward pass.

The caveat that bounds this: it holds only where the environment supplies a discrete admissible set. For open-ended text actions there is nothing to score, generation returns, and the probe becomes a genuine additive cost.

The cheap rung beat the expensive one

Summarised memory on a frozen φ is the only arm that beats plain GRPO, and it does so on the benchmark where the arms differ most. Every learned component has so far failed to earn its cost: learned φ is the worst arm on two of three benchmarks, and the learned affinity — once given a real objective — scores ρ=0.515 against plain Euclidean's ρ=0.514.

That is the plan's ablation ladder returning its intended answer: build the cheap rung first, and only pay for the learned one if it wins. Here it does not.

Two caveats that bound every ALFWorld number above

Single task type. Task listing returned range(n) over a sorted game list, which clusters by type — the first twelve games are all examine, the easiest of six. Train and eval were disjoint trials within that one type. Cross-type transfer — precisely where "same observation, different meaning" should matter — remains untested. Fixed with stratified sampling; the runs need redoing.

Held-out peaks then declines. ALFWorld arms peak near iteration 20–25 and fall by up to −0.29 afterwards, so the final checkpoint is the wrong one to keep and the final-iteration table understates every step-level arm. Checkpointing now saves best-on-held-out.

05c — 2026-08-29 diagnostic: where the term is actually live

The context term is inert on sparse ALFWorld at 1.7B

λu is the MSE-optimal shrinkage toward the observation-only baseline; it is driven to 0 whenever the other trajectories in a bucket have identical returns (s²=0), because then conditioning has no bias to remove. Measured per iteration over the 08-27 runs:

Consequence. On ALFWorld our estimator is GRPO plus a few percent of noise until success is high enough to populate buckets with mixed outcomes; it cannot beat GRPO there by construction at the 1.7B success level. The levers that can matter are the dense-reward benchmarks with the corrected scale and the leak-free progress term. ScienceWorld metric. The simulator returns −100 and terminates on the fail state (≈40 % of training episodes); no GRPO-family release contains a ScienceWorld wrapper, and the ScienceWorld paper normalises scores to [0,1]. We report mean score with failures floored at 0 as the publication metric (signed score and strict wins alongside) and select checkpoints on the same quantity.

The matched-scale run has been through two full generations. Eval protocol — a correction

verl maps eval_in_distribution → valid_seen and eval_out_of_distribution → valid_unseen, and defaults to the former. G²PO's run_alfworld.sh never overrides it, so their reported numbers are on the seen split; ours are on unseen. We expected that to explain a large part of the gap to published results — the ALFWorld literature typically shows 10–25 points between the splits.

It does not. The v5 best checkpoint scores 0.312 on valid_seen against 0.320 on valid_unseen — no meaningful gap. Our result is split-insensitive, and the distance to published numbers is real rather than a protocol artefact. What remains different: backbone, training length, and the use_remove_padding + flash-attn stack unavailable on sm_120.

06 — where it stands

Is the expensive run justified?

Partly — and the answer is narrower than the original idea.

Two things are established. The target effect is real: within-bucket credit heterogeneity is significant on two of three benchmarks, worth a quarter to a third of a standard deviation of baseline bias — though modest at 3–5% excess explained variance. And the probe-stationarity fix works, replicated three times.

The blocker is the central hypothesis itself. Across two environments, four φ variants and four arms, no correlation between the predictive proxy and the credit oracle survives a null-representation control. That is not a refutation — four separate measurement defects had to be fixed before the question could even be asked, and the first clean attempt is underpowered at 15–22 usable pairs.

The training runs answer it in one direction: a context key carrying summarised memory beats plain GRPO on held-out ALFWorld (0.625 vs 0.604, peak 0.917 vs 0.667), while every learned component under-performs the frozen version it was meant to improve. So the defensible claim is the narrow one — memory-bearing context helps — not the original one about learned predictive equivalence.

The blocking experiment is now the stratified ALFWorld rerun: every number above was measured inside a single task type, and cross-type transfer is exactly the regime the method is designed for. Until that lands, treat the ALFWorld column as within-type transfer and nothing more.

Update — 2026-08-29. The corrected 50-step round with clean-protocol adjudication has since moved the picture: WebShop is a statistical tie on the 488 untouched test goals (greedy 0.301 GRPO / 0.296 ours / 0.290 ours+cc-norm, gaps ≤ 1 SE); ALFWorld splits by seed (seed 0: ours 0.27 in-training vs GRPO 0.066 clean; seed 1 leans GRPO); ScienceWorld leans GRPO. The §05c diagnostic explains the pattern — the context term is structurally inert on sparse ALFWorld at this success level — and probes showed think-then-score lifts ALFWorld base success 2.4× while hurting the other envs. On that basis all six GPUs were redirected (user directive) to the §05d matched-scale run: our estimator inside verl-agent with free generation and the chain-of-thought trained by the RL gradient, in Docker on the Blackwell cards, 150 steps; the GRPO baseline follows in the identical configuration. That run — not the stratified rerun — is now the blocking experiment.
