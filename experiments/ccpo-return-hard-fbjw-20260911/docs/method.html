The Unmatched Turns

Under an exact-state gate, 8–10% of turns have no sibling rollout to be compared against and receive no step credit at all, while the rest are compared against baselines built from as few as one other trajectory. This arm credits the first group against the task, and down-weights the second by how much evidence it has. It is the fastest-learning arm we have run, and it ends where its control ends.

01 — the two flags

One fills a hole, the other prices a risk

This arm is ccpo-return-hard plus exactly two environment variables. Everything else — return-to-go target, exact-state grouping, uniform within-node weights, edge term, raw anchor, seed, hardware, backend — is identical, so the comparison is paired.

1
if J[i] = 0: b[i] = mean of other trajectories' returns in the whole task # ACG_CCPO_BACKOFF_TASK=1
J is the number of other trajectories that visited this exact state. When it is zero there is no leave-one-out comparison to make, and the turn previously got a step advantage of exactly zero — it trained on the episode and edge terms alone. The fallback credits it against the task bucket, the same pool the global gate uses. Only those turns are touched; every matched turn is bit-identical to the control.
2
A_CC[i] ← A_CC[i] · J[i] / (J[i] + 1) # ACG_CCPO_JWEIGHT_C=1.0
The share of an advantage that is baseline noise rather than signal is ≈1/(J+1), so J/(J+1) is the reliability weight, with c=1 derived rather than tuned. It scales the step term only; the edge and episode terms are untouched.
Two changes, so the arm cannot attribute

A positive result here does not say which flag did it. The offline work predicted the fallback would matter more (it reaches turns with no credit) and the weight less (it rescales turns that already have a baseline). §03 shows the weight is close to a constant in practice, which supports that ordering but does not prove it. The next rung replaces the weight with a change to the baseline itself (H-AK, ccpo-return-hard-cred).

Support is bimodal. 60.0% of turns are matched by all 7 siblings and another 10.1% by 6, so for seven turns in ten the weight is a flat 0.86–0.88 — a global rescale the per-task standardisation largely undoes. Only the 17% at J≤3 are meaningfully damped. This is the arm's own evidence that the fallback, not the weight, is the live half.

Fallback turns carry ~1.45× the credit magnitude of matched turns, and their mean credit grows steadily more negative: the task mean sits above what off-path states actually return. A baseline that does not depend on the action leaves the policy gradient unbiased, so this is variance, not bias — but it is variance concentrated exactly where the next rung aims. A turn-matched prior does not fix it: tested offline, dead-row error rose 7.59 → 8.14.

Ahead early and in the middle, identical at the end. Two independent measurements of the same phenomenon.

noise yardstick15 points

The resume gave us a free replicate

A disk-full crash at step 15 forced a resume from the step-10 checkpoint. Steps 11–14 therefore ran twice from identical weights, differing only in sampling: train success at step 14 was 26.6% the first time and 11.7% the second. Any single-step comparison in this project must clear that bar.

pairingverified

Step 1 matched to four decimals

Both arms scored 0.1406 train success at step 1: same seed, same games, same base weights, and an estimator that only changes the update. The pairing this comparison rests on is real.

What this arm does not show

It does not raise the ceiling. Step 100 is 82.0 against the control's 84.4, and the window gap is the size of same-config noise. The honest summary is: equal endpoint, reached sooner, with a cleaner middle. For a 100-step budget that is worth something; for the published comparison at 95.0 it is not the missing piece. The held-out failures remain exploration failures — agents looping over 2–3 receptacles — and no change to the credit assigner addresses those.

The crash, and what was done about it

At step 15 the checkpoint save failed with PytorchStreamWriter failed writing file — the volume was full, because pruning runs after the save. 101 GB was freed, the run was resumed from global_step_10, and the trimmed steps 11–14 were preserved in outputs/pre-crash/. Consequences to keep in mind when reading this run: the optimizer state carried over cleanly, but the rollouts of steps 11–14 are not the ones the first attempt drew — which is what produced the replicate in §05. The trainer now takes ACG_KEEP_CKPTS so a run can hold one rolling checkpoint instead of two.

08 — what comes next

Replace the weight with a better baseline

§03 showed the weight is nearly constant and the fallback is the live half. The next rung keeps the idea — trust a comparison in proportion to the evidence behind it — but applies it to the baseline rather than to the finished advantage.

→
b = λ·b_node + (1−λ)·b_task, λ = J / (J + κ), κ = 2
The J=0 case of this formula is exactly the fallback, so one mechanism replaces two flags. Offline on this arm's control: baseline error −6.0%, better on 100/100 steps, and −29% on turns matched by a single rollout. Running as ccpo-return-hard-cred-20260911.
